Technical health

One CWV point worth knowing before you measure

Field data almost certainly does not exist for this origin. The Chrome UX Report needs a minimum traffic threshold to publish an origin, and 37 impressions in 38 days is nowhere near it. PageSpeed Insights will therefore only ever return lab (Lighthouse) numbers here. Make sure nobody reports "no Core Web Vitals issues detected" as a pass — that message means no data, not no problem. Judge performance on the Lighthouse run and on WebPageTest until real traffic exists.

The subdomain is a self-inflicted handicap

This is the one architectural call I will make without a crawl, because it does not need one. school.orbittechlab.com is a separate host from www.orbittechlab.com. Google consolidates authority far more readily within a single hostname than across subdomains, and you are currently asking a brand-new product on a subdomain of a brand-new studio domain to earn trust twice, from zero, in parallel.

Three options, in order of my preference:

  1. orbittechlab.com/campuscore/ — a subfolder. Every link the studio earns for ToolOrbit, Repliva or Orbit AI compounds into CampusCore's ranking ability, and vice versa. For a multi-product studio this is almost always correct.
  2. A dedicated exact-match domain (campuscore.pk or similar) — weaker on consolidation, but a .pk ccTLD is a genuine geo-relevance signal in this market and would help the brand-collision problem in section 6.
  3. Stay on the subdomain — only defensible if a migration would break the app's routing or auth in ways that cost more than the SEO gain.

Decide this now. You have two ranking URLs and one click to lose. Migrating today costs a weekend. Migrating in eighteen months, with a few hundred ranking pages and real traffic, costs a quarter of lost revenue and a redirect map nobody wants to own.

Content and E-E-A-T

You left the competitor field blank, so I pulled the set that actually ranks for the category in this market: EduSuite, Capobrain and TaleemPro, with Sowaan ERP and Futuresol Cloud Campus ERP close behind, plus the aggregator PakEducate. Confirm this is your real competitive set.

The content gaps that decide this category

These are not "topics you could cover". They are the specific things Pakistani school buyers filter on, that your competitors have pages for and you do not:

  1. FBR-compliant invoicing. Both EduSuite and Cloud Campus ERP lead with tax integration. For a Pakistani school this is a procurement blocker, not a feature. If CampusCore handles it, there is no page saying so — and if it does not, that is a product gap disguised as an SEO gap.
  2. Named payment integrations — JazzCash, EasyPaisa, SadaPay, HBL. These carry the highest commercial intent in the niche, because a school searching "school fee software JazzCash integration" has already decided to buy and is checking compatibility. Each integration deserves its own page. This is the single largest addressable gap on the list.
  3. WhatsApp and SMS parent communication. EduSuite ships it; TaleemPro competes on it. Pakistani parent expectations here are specific and worth a dedicated page.
  4. Urdu / bilingual interface. TaleemPro's explicit wedge is full Urdu–English support. If CampusCore has it, say so loudly; if not, know that you are losing a comparison you never see.
  5. Published PKR pricing. EduSuite publishes tiers with a free entry point. Buyers in this market filter on price before they filter on features — a "contact us for pricing" page removes you from the shortlist before anyone speaks to you.
  6. Comparison and listicle content. edusuite.pk, taleempro.pk and pakeducate.com all run "best school management software in Pakistan" pieces that rank. This is how the category is discovered. CampusCore appears in none of them — not as a listing, and not with a competing page of its own.

E-E-A-T: you have a real asset and you are not using it

The studio positioning — an independent Lahore software house that builds and operates its own products, and depends on them internally — is a genuine trust signal, and it is differentiated in a category full of anonymous ERP vendors. It does not currently appear to be doing any work.

The signals this category is broadly missing, which means they are cheap to win:

  • Named founders and engineers with real bios, on an About page and as post authors.
  • A verifiable school count and named school case studies — "used by 40 schools" is worth less than one named school with a headmaster quote.
  • A physical Lahore address, phone number and company registration details in the footer. In a market with real vendor-trust anxiety, this converts and it corroborates the entity.
  • Dated content with visible last-updated stamps.

The entity problem is the AI problem verified

This is the most important finding in the report, and it explains the brand-query data in section 5. Ask any model or AI Overview "what is CampusCore" and there are at least six competing answers, none of which are yours:

You are not losing the brand SERP because your homepage is weak. You are losing it because "CampusCore" is not a distinctive name in this space and you have supplied Google with nothing to tell you apart. Until the entity is disambiguated, you will not be cited by AI Overviews, ChatGPT, Perplexity or Gemini for your own name — they have six better-established candidates.

Two things follow. Short term, disambiguate hard with schema and sameAs (below). Long term, have an honest conversation about the name — the .pk domain option in section 2 exists partly to solve this, and "CampusCore Pakistan" or a rename is cheaper now than after you have brand equity worth keeping.

Exactly which schema to add, in priority order

  1. Organization — sitewide, in the <head>. Name "Orbit Tech Lab", url, logo, address (the Lahore address), founder, and critically sameAs pointing to every profile you control: LinkedIn, X, Facebook, Crunchbase, and the App Store / Play Store listings if they are yours. This is the disambiguation anchor — everything else hangs off it.
  2. SoftwareApplication — on the homepage. name: "CampusCore", applicationCategory: "BusinessApplication", operatingSystem, offers with priceCurrency: "PKR" and real prices, areaServed: "PK", and publisher referencing the Organization node. The areaServed and PKR pricing are what separate you from the six other CampusCores.
  3. WebSite with SearchAction — enables the sitelinks search box and reinforces the site-level entity.
  4. FAQPage — on the homepage, the pricing page and each feature page. This is the highest-yield schema for AI Overview citation, because it maps a question to an answer explicitly and models lift that pairing directly.
  5. BreadcrumbList — sitewide. Cheap, and it is one of the few rich results a young site can actually win.
  6. Article with a real author — on every blog post, where author is a Person node with name, jobTitle and sameAs. Anonymous posts earn no E-E-A-T credit.

Do not add LocalBusiness. You are a software studio selling nationally, not a premises customers visit. Organization is the correct type here, and using LocalBusiness would send a geographic signal that misrepresents the business.

Page structure for citation

I cannot verify the current state of any page, so treat this as the target, not a diff. Each key page needs a 40–60 word direct-answer paragraph immediately below the H1 that defines the entity in plain declarative terms — "CampusCore is a school management system for Pakistani schools that handles fee collection, attendance, exams and parent communication across five role-based portals." Then question-form H2s that match real queries, and factual claims carrying dates and sources. Language models lift definitional paragraphs and question-answer pairs; they skip hero marketing copy entirely.

Five realistic opportunities, ordered by realism

  1. The aggregator listicles that already rank. PakEducate's "Best School Management System Pakistan 2026: Top 10 Compared" is a neutral roundup ranking for the exact money query. Getting listed is a link and qualified referral traffic from a buyer already comparing vendors. Pitch with a one-page spec sheet, published pricing and screenshots — roundup editors reject vendors who make them dig.
  2. Vendor directories. Capterra, SoftwareSuggest, SourceForge, GetApp, Crunchbase. Free listings, they rank in this category, and each one doubles as a sameAs target that corroborates your entity for section 6. Best effort-to-value ratio on this list by some distance.
  3. The parent domain. orbittechlab.com already ranks and has an About page describing the product line. A prominent contextual link from the homepage and About page into CampusCore costs nothing and ships today. Cannot verify whether it already exists — check first.
  4. Pakistani tech press. TechJuice, ProPakistani, Startup Pakistan. An independent Lahore studio shipping a school ERP against established vendors is a legitimate local story, and the founder angle carries it further than a product announcement would.
  5. Named school case studies. Every school you deploy to is a potential link — school sites routinely link to their management portal — plus a testimonial and an E-E-A-T signal. Ask at deployment, while goodwill is highest; asking twelve months later almost never works.

Explicitly avoid: guest-post networks and paid directory packages aimed at this niche. Pakistani edtech has a dense low-quality directory ecosystem that will take your money and move nothing.

Measure the right thing

At a baseline of one click per 38 days, judging this programme on clicks for the next 90 days will tell you nothing — the noise is larger than any plausible signal. Track these instead, in this order:

  1. Indexed page count (GSC → Pages). Moves first, within days.
  2. Impressions per day, 7-day rolling. The flat line in the chart above is the number that has to bend.
  3. Count of distinct queries returning impressions. Breadth before volume.
  4. Average position for campuscore. Position 1 is the target and it is entirely winnable — it just needs the entity work, not more content.
